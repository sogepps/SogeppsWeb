How to Set Up a Single Elimination Tournament Bracket?

League & Tournament Maker 27 August 2026·2 min read

Single elimination tournament is the fastest format: each match eliminates half the participants, no one plays extra matches. But if you set up the bracket wrong, the tournament becomes lopsided by the first round.

Participant count should be a power of two

A single elimination bracket works cleanest with 4, 8, 16, or 32 participants. At these numbers, each round divides evenly in half and no one gets a bye.

  • 4 participants → 2 rounds (semifinal, final)
  • 8 participants → 3 rounds
  • 16 participants → 4 rounds
  • 32 participants → 5 rounds

If the number doesn't work out: byes

If the participant count isn't a power of two, some teams skip the first round without playing; this is called a bye. The number of byes is found by subtracting the participant count from the next power of two above it.

For example, with 12 participants, the next power of two is 16; 16 − 12 = 4 teams get byes. Byes aren't random but go to the top-ranked teams — otherwise the format becomes unfair within itself.

Why does seeding placement matter?

If top seeds are randomly placed on the bracket, the two strongest teams might meet in the first round and the tournament's final gets played early. With correct seeding, the 1st seed goes at the top of the bracket, the 2nd at the bottom; that way they only meet in the final.

By the same logic, the 3rd and 4th seeds are placed so they meet the 1st and 2nd in the semifinals.

Single match or double match?

  • Single elimination: The fastest. One bad day is enough to get knocked out — good for short tournaments.
  • Two-leg tie (home and away): Reduces luck's impact but doubles the number of matches.
  • Double elimination: Lose once and drop to the lower bracket; lose twice and you're out. The fairest but longest format.

Should a third-place match be played?

A third-place match between the two semifinal losers adds one match but gives participants a chance to finish with a win. Usually not played in small groups; in tournaments with prizes, it's played because ranking is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many byes in a 12-person tournament?

The next power of two is 16, so 16 − 12 = 4 participants get a bye in the first round. Bye spots go to the top-ranked teams.

How are seeded teams placed on the bracket?

1st seed goes at the top, 2nd at the bottom; 3rd and 4th are placed so they only meet 1st and 2nd in the semifinals. This keeps strong teams from early elimination.

When should double elimination be used?

When you don't want a single bad match to end the tournament. It gives a fairer result but significantly increases match count and time.
