Winning Strategies in Backgammon: 7 Rules for Beginners

SogeGammon 27 August 2026·2 min read

Everyone gets the same dice in backgammon; what makes the difference is what you do with the roll. The seven principles below are the rules that fastest raise a beginner's win rate.

1. Don't leave single pieces (blots)

A single piece on a point can be hit and sent back to the start. Ask yourself one question each turn: "After this move, how many of my pieces are exposed, and what rolls will my opponent need to hit them?"

Leaving blots is not always wrong, but what you gain must be bigger than the risk.

2. Try to close your home board

Closing points in your home board (last six points) with two pieces makes it hard for captured opponent pieces to re-enter. As you close more points, the time your opponent spends stuck off the board grows.

Especially closing point 5 (the golden point) is considered one of backgammon's most valuable single moves.

3. Time your piece captures

  • If your opponent's home board is well blocked, capturing is more valuable; they'll struggle to re-enter.
  • If your own home board is open, you're likely to get hit back after capturing.
  • Hitting when ahead is less risky than when behind.

4. Running game or holding game?

If you're ahead on the race, reducing contact helps you: move your pieces quickly home and avoid meeting them. This is called a running game.

If you're behind, do the opposite: keep pieces on your opponent's path and wait for hitting chances. For a trailing player, contact is the only realistic way to win; gathering in early locks in defeat.

5. Don't clump pieces

Stacking six or seven pieces on one point is a wasted resource. Spread pieces give you more opportunities to close points; clumped pieces limit your move options.

6. Know basic dice odds roughly

  • Two dice give 36 different outcomes.
  • The most likely roll to hit a distance is 6 (about 17/36).
  • The chance of being hit from one space away is 11/36 — that's roughly one in three rolls.
  • Far distances (greater than 7) are only hit by combinations, probability drops sharply.

7. In the end game, do the math

When pieces leave contact, the game becomes a pure race. The only question then is: who will take fewer rolls to gather? Roughly counting remaining distance tells you whether you need risky moves or can play safely.

The fastest way to practice

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most important point in backgammon?

Your own home board's point 5. Closing it early directly affects both defense and your opponent's re-entry, making it the single most valuable point.

What should you do when you're behind?

Instead of avoiding contact, seek it out. Keeping pieces on your opponent's path and waiting for hitting chances is the only realistic way to come from behind.

Is leaving a blot always bad?

No. Taking risk to close a point or gain an important position is often the right move; the critical thing is to calculate what the risk is worth.
