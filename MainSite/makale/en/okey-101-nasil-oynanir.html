How to Play Okey 101? Differences from Classic Okey

Metin2 Okey 3 September 2026·3 min read

Okey 101 is the longer, more strategic cousin of classic Okey. It uses the same 106 tiles but has a threshold for the first hand: you can't open unless your tiles add up to at least 101 points. This single rule is what really makes the game different.

Basic setup

  • Players: 4 people.
  • Tiles: Same as classic Okey — two sets each of 1–13 in four colors, plus two jokers; total 106.
  • Deal: Each player gets 21 tiles, the starting player gets 22.
  • Indicator and joker: Determined the same way as in classic — the joker tile is the next one up from the indicator's color.

The 101 rule: opening threshold

The game's name comes from here. To open a run on the table, the total value of the tiles in your opening run must be at least 101 points . If you can't reach this threshold, you keep your tiles.

In scoring, tiles count by their number values. Details (how much a 1 tile counts, joker value, double run situations) are house rules that vary table to table; clarifying this before starting prevents the most common arguments.

Valid runs

  • Sequence: At least three consecutive tiles of the same color — yellow 5, yellow 6, yellow 7.
  • Group: At least three tiles of the same number in different colors — blue 9, black 9, red 9.
  • The same color cannot appear twice in a group.
  • A joker tile substitutes for any tile; a fake joker substitutes for a joker.

Laying on: after opening

The game changes after you open your hand. Now you can add tiles to both your own runs and other players' runs on the table. This is called laying on and is the main way to empty your hand quickly.

Classic Okey has no such phase; the game is based on finishing in one go. In 101, there's a separate game between opening and finishing — and most matches are won and lost here.

Finishing and scoring

  • The player who converts all their tiles to runs and plays their last tile wins the hand.
  • Players who can't finish take penalty points equal to the value of tiles left in hand.
  • Points work downward: players start from a starting score and whoever gets their score to zero first wins — or whoever accumulates the fewest penalty points, depending on the table's preference.
  • Penalty point calculation and starting score also vary by house rules; discuss it beforehand.

Differences between classic Okey and 101

  • Number of tiles: 14 in classic, 21 in 101.
  • Opening: No opening in classic; 101 has a 101-point threshold.
  • Laying on: None in classic, present in 101.
  • Duration: Classic hand lasts a few minutes, 101 hand is noticeably longer.
  • Strategy: Speed matters in classic, planning in 101.

Three tips for beginners

  • Target the opening threshold early; opening late means you never get to use the laying-on phase.
  • Don't hoard high-value tiles (10-11-12-13) — if you can't finish, your penalty points balloon.
  • Saving your joker for the finish instead of using it at opening pays off in most hands.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tiles are dealt in Okey 101?

Each player gets 21 tiles, the starting player gets 22. Noticeably more than the 14 in classic Okey.

What does 101 points mean?

To open a run on the table, the total value of the tiles in your opening run must be at least 101. You can't open without reaching this threshold.

What's the main difference between Okey 101 and classic Okey?

Two big differences: in 101, 21 tiles are dealt and there's a 101-point opening threshold; also, after opening you have a "laying on" phase where you can add tiles to runs on the table.
