Match Prediction Game: Set Up a Score Prediction League with Friends

Tahmin Meleği - İddaa September 3, 2026·2 min read

This content is for readers 18 and older. It is for informational and entertainment purposes; it is not advice about betting, alcohol consumption, or similar topics.

Match prediction works without money too, and honestly it's more fun that way. A prediction league in a friend group creates conversation for the whole week, and the only thing the loser loses is bragging rights.

How to set it up?

  • Determine participants. 4–10 people is ideal; larger groups make tracking weekly scores harder.
  • Pick a week. 5–10 matches per week is enough. Don't try to cover the entire league.
  • Set a deadline for predictions. The start time of the first match is the limit; predictions aren't accepted after that.
  • Keep predictions hidden. They should be revealed only after everyone has submitted, otherwise people copy the next ones.
  • Keep the score in one place. The table should be visible; an invisible score kills interest.

Scoring system

A simple but balanced system keeps everyone playing. Suggested distribution:

  • Correct exact score: 5 points
  • Correct match result (1/X/2): 3 points
  • Correct over/under on goals: 2 points
  • Correct both sides to score: 1 point
  • None: 0 points — don't assign negative points, it drives people out of the game

Prediction categories

Just doing score predictions gets boring after a while. Additional categories that bring color to the week:

  • Team to score first
  • Total cards over/under
  • Week's surprise result — double points if you guess it
  • Joker match: pick one match a week and double your points for it

Three rules that keep the league alive

  • Keep seasons short. An 8–10 week season completes at a much higher rate than a 34-week one.
  • Don't punish latecomers, they just miss that week. Penalty points drive people out of the game.
  • Announce weekly winners. If there's only overall ranking, those behind early give up fast.

Don't bring money into it

The whole appeal of this game is that there's no betting. The moment money appears, the game changes: people start talking about losses, not predictions, and the group dynamic suffers.

If rewards are wanted, pick something non-monetary: a coffee the losers buy at the end of the season, or giving the last-place finisher the right to choose the group profile picture for a week.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the match prediction game scored?

Common distribution is exact score 5, match result 3, over/under 2, both sides to score 1 point. Not giving negative points keeps participation going throughout the season.

How many people play?

4–10 people is ideal. In larger groups, tracking weekly scores gets harder and interest drops because the table doesn't get updated.

Should money be put in?

Not recommended. The moment money enters, the character of the game changes. Non-monetary symbolic rewards provide the same motivation in a much healthier way.
